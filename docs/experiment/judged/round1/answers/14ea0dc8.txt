**Short answer:** after a DoP track, a pause or stop sends **DoP silence**, not PCM zeros, for as long as the device stays at that track's rate. That includes the idle release, right up to the close. Nothing needs fixing, and the ledger tells reviews not to reopen this topic.

**What the code does** (`engine/output_coreaudio.c`):

- `_write_frames` (line 1122 on) fills silence from `silencebuf`. When `_silence_fmt()` returns anything other than PCM, it switches to `silencebuf_dsd` and adds DoP markers with `update_dop`. The result is 0x05/0xFA marker bytes over a 0x6969 payload.
- `_silence_fmt()` (lines 1098–1102) picks the format in this order:
  1. If a track is pending and the device is already at its rate, it uses that track's format (`output.next_fmt`).
  2. Otherwise, if the device is at the rate of the last track start the IOProc took, it uses that track's format (`ca.track_rate` / `ca.track_fmt`). A pause or stop after DoP lands here, so the silence is DoP.
  3. Anything else gets PCM zeros. This is deliberate: DoP silence at a rate no DoP track plays at reaches the DAC as a square wave at Nyquist, about −27 dBFS.
- `ca.track_rate` and `ca.track_fmt` are set only in `ioproc` (lines 1213–1216), when output.c takes a track start. A stop's `output_flush` therefore can't trick it into DoP silence at 44.1 kHz. That was fixed in CODE-REVIEW 2026-10-11 (3).
- While the output is `OUTPUT_OFF` (lines 1195–1204), the IOProc still calls `_output_frames`. So the idle release also ends in DoP silence. Before 0.2.10 it ended in 2.9 ms of PCM zeros. This was verified live on the FiiO on 2026-10-11 with the output tap, which captures the frames sent to the DAC: no zero frames at all in the 176.4 kHz capture, and DoP silence to the close.

**Why it isn't a bug to chase:** the CLAUDE.md ledger settles this as `SILENCE FORMAT IS NOT A FINDING`, decided by Simon on 2026-10-11. Which format the silence takes (when stopped, paused, buffering, around an open or at the idle release), and claims that the DAC "loses its DSD lock" there, are not to be reported or reworked.

- The idea that DSD silence keeps the DAC locked comes from upstream squeezelite and was never tested on Simon's DAC.
- Simon switched between most formats himself, including the same-rate DSD128/DSD256 pair, and found no problem.
- Earlier review rounds kept refining this rule on paper and kept turning up new findings.

The engine was kept frame for frame as 0.2.10, which already gives DoP silence after DoP.

**What would still count as a bug:**
- DoP silence at a rate no DoP track plays at. Rule 3 above prevents that.
- Anything Simon actually hears on the DAC.

If you have a concrete audible symptom, that would reopen it. Otherwise the current behaviour already does what your question asks for.
